# Sam's app — plan

> Working doc. A **light, hot-pink sibling to KILOS** for Sam: her own
> postpartum lean-&-toned program, rebuilt **time-based** (the app paces and
> counts; she never counts), on her own home-screen icon with her own coach
> voice.
>
> **Name locked 2026-08-05: the app is HOTMUM; the program is SAM'S TONED
> CHRISTMAS** (§1.1, §2.0). MUM vs MOM still open, and Sam still has to say yes
> to the name itself.

---

## 1. The name

Brief: **hot mama**. English — the Tagalog requirement is dropped. Must work as
a wordmark in condensed caps, survive as an icon on her home screen, and not
read as a beauty app.

**The real decision isn't the word — it's the register.** The name sits on her
phone forever and is visible to anyone who glances at it. Explicit is funnier
in the group chat; implied ages better in public. Pick the register first, then
the word.

### Register 1 — Say it

| Name | Why | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **HOTMUM / HOTMOM** | Closed-up, one word, literally the brief. See §1.1 — it's the front-runner. | Only name here that's purely about looks |
| **MOTHERLOAD** | mother + *the load*. The misspelling IS the joke. Self-aware, warm, strong wordmark. | Everyone gets it instantly — including strangers |
| **MAMA** | Bold simplicity. Huge in Teko caps, warm, universal, zero cringe. | Generic alone; the brand does the work |

### 1.1 HOTMUM — the case for and against

**For, and these are stronger than they first look:**

- **It stacks into a perfect icon.** `HOT` over `MUM` is two lines of three
  letters — a square block in Teko, no abbreviation, no monogram, no
  compromise. `HOT`/`MOM` is even better balanced (the two O's rhyme). This
  beats the `KO` mark, and it beats initials: **`HM` is unusable — it reads as
  H&M.**
- **It matches the reference posters better than anything else proposed.**
  `GIRLS JUST WANNA RUN` is playful, declarative, slightly cheeky. HOTMUM is
  that exact register. KNOCKOUT is a colder, harder brand than those images.
- **"MUM" ties to the coach voice.** Alice is British. British spelling makes
  that a deliberate bit rather than a coincidence.
- **It's his name for her.** For a private app built by a husband for his wife,
  an affectionate in-joke beats a clever agency name. That's the actual
  emotional core of this thing.
- Most branding objections (search, app-store collision, longevity) simply
  don't apply — nobody is downloading this but her.

**Against — one real objection:**

Every other candidate carries a second meaning about *effort or strength*:
KNOCKOUT is a fighter, MOTHERLOAD is the load, TEMPO is the work. HOTMUM has
one meaning, and it's about how she looks. Postpartum, that register is
load-bearing: a name that only references attractiveness can quietly reframe the
whole app as an appearance project rather than a strength one — which is the
opposite of what the program is (§2: progressive overload, not a shrink plan).

**The fix is one line of copy, not a different name.** Put the reframe in the
tagline so the name gets its second meaning back:

> **HOTMUM — *hot as in strong.***

**And the one thing that isn't ours to decide:** it goes on her home screen,
visible to anyone who glances at her phone. Some women would love that, some
would be mortified. Ask her before it's built.

**MUM or MOM?** `MUM` is British — coherent with Alice, and the more distinctive
of the two. `MOM` is the natural register in the Philippines and gives the
better-balanced wordmark. Her call; both stack identically.

> **Steal this regardless of the name:** during a tempo set, pulse the magenta

**Blocking question for her, not for us:** it lives on her home screen where
anyone can see it. That's a yes-or-no only she can give.

## 2. The program, converted to time

### 2.0 Two names, two things

**HOTMUM is the app. SAM'S TONED CHRISTMAS is the program inside it.** Same
relationship KILOS has with Density 40 and the rehab protocol — the app outlives
any one block of training.

That makes the program a **season**, and seasons have a start and an end:

> **SEASON 01 :: TONED CHRISTMAS — 20 weeks, Aug → Dec, ending 25 Dec 2026.**

Starting Tue 11 Aug gives **19.4 weeks**; from today it's **142 days**. Call it
a 20-week block — which is a genuinely well-sized training cycle, not a number
picked to fit the joke.

**Why the deadline is the best thing to happen to this app:**

- It replaces the streak. A countdown to a fixed date motivates without
  punishing a missed day, which matters enormously with a newborn (§7 had this
  as an open question — the season answers it).
- It has an **end**. She isn't signing up for forever, and there's a defined
  moment to celebrate and decide what Season 02 is.
- It gives the poster grammar real content. The reference poster's own device is
  a date range — `APRIL – OCTOBER`. Hers is `AUG — DEC`, `WK 04 OF 20`,
  `142 DAYS`. The identity and the programming say the same thing.

**Do NOT make the palette Christmassy.** No red and green. The countdown is the
only Christmas reference; magenta/ember/cream stays exactly as it is.

### 2.0.1 Periodisation across the 20 weeks

Fixed 10/15/20 lb dumbbells mean load is a blunt lever (§2.1), so the season
progresses through tempo and volume first, and load only where the jump is
survivable. Five four-week blocks:

| Block | Weeks | Name | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–4 | **GROOVE** | Her plan exactly as written. Learn the tempos, 3×10. |
| 2 | 5–8 | **EXTEND** | Reps 10 → 12 at the same tempo. A 50s set becomes 60s. |
| 3 | 9–12 | **SLOW** | Lengthen the eccentric (`3-1-1` → `4-1-1`), reps back to 10. |
| 4 | 13–16 | **LOAD** | 15 → 20 lb on hinge, thrust and squat only. Reps drop to 8. |
| 5 | 17–20 | **PEAK** | Best tempo + best load, 4th set on the main lift. Week 20 lighter. |

**No scheduled deload.** With a newborn the miss rate supplies it. Instead: if
she completes three full weeks back-to-back, the app *offers* an easy week —
suggested, never imposed.

### 2.0.2 Walk days

**30-minute walks on the four days between sessions** — Mon, Wed, Fri, Sun. With
the three lifting days that's something every day, which is the whole retention
mechanic: the app becomes a daily open rather than a thrice-weekly one.

It's also the right training call. Walking is the NEAT lever that actually moves
body composition alongside lifting, it's the one thing that stays possible on no
sleep, and it's stroller-compatible.

**Build it as small as it deserves:**

- A 30:00 countdown that survives the screen locking, plus a **"log it"** tap for
  walks already done — she'll often walk without opening the app first.
- **No GPS, no map, no route, no step count.** That's a different app, it breaks
  the offline/local-first model, and it turns a two-tap habit into a chore.
- Walks count toward the season rail but are visibly lighter weight than a
  session — a walk is not a workout and the UI shouldn't pretend otherwise.

### 2.1 The rule

Her plan is written in sets × reps. She trains better on time. So:

> **A set is a countdown, not a count.** Set length = `reps × seconds-per-rep`.
> The coach speaks the tempo (*lift… squeeze… lower*) and **says the rep number
> out loud** on every rep. She moves with the voice and the set ends when it
> ends.

She gets the time-based feel *and* keeps the rep accountability — the app does
the counting. This is not a new mechanism: the KILOS guided player already does
exactly this (`mode: 'tempo'` in [rehab.js](src/workout/rehab.js), rep counting
added in commit `4c7328d`).

**It also fixes a real problem in her plan.** She owns fixed 10/15/20 lb
dumbbells. Going 15 → 20 lb is a **33% jump** — far too big to be the next
step on a lunge. With fixed weights, the only sane progression lever is
**time under tension**, which is precisely what a tempo makes adjustable.
Progression order: *tempo → reps → sets → load*, load last.

### 2.2 Notation

Written as `lower–pause–lift` seconds, e.g. RDL `3-1-1` = 5s per rep. In code
this is the shape the engine already takes:

```js
{ ex: 'rdl', mode: 'tempo', sets: 3, reps: 10,
  tempo: [['LOWER', 3], ['PAUSE', 1], ['LIFT', 1]] }   // → a 50s set
```

### 2.7 Session length — and the honest trade-off

Tempo sets take **longer than rushed sets**. That's the point — more time under
tension at a light fixed load — but it costs real minutes. So the session picker
offers **three doses**, chosen at the start. Same program, three exits: she
never has to choose between "all of it" and "nothing", the failure mode that
actually kills consistency.

**MEASURED, not estimated** (`npm test` computes these from the data, and the
suite guards the bands):

| Session | FULL | SHORT | CORE | Under tension | Sets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lower A | **41 min** | 28 min | 6 min | 26 min | 32 |
| Upper | **46 min** | 25 min | 7 min | 28 min | 37 |
| Lower B | **43 min** | 31 min | 5 min | 27 min | 36 |

**These are longer than the 34–40 min first estimated.** The honest read: her
plan is 9 exercises plus core, and under tempo that's a 41–46 minute session.
Nothing is padded — 26–28 of those minutes are actual time under tension.

**Two open decisions this surfaces:**

1. **Is 45 minutes viable with a newborn?** If not, the fix is hers to choose —
   drop a finisher, or make SHORT the default and treat FULL as the good-day
   option. Don't silently trim her plan.
2. **SHORT doesn't work on Lower B.** It saves 46% on Upper but only 28% on
   Lower B, landing at 31 min — nowhere near the ~22 the dose implies, because
   Lower B's main block is enormous. Cleanest fix: re-tier the **step-up** as a
   finisher (it duplicates the reverse-lunge pattern already covered that day),
   which brings SHORT to ~25 min. That's a programming call, so it's flagged
   rather than applied.

### 2.8 Postpartum layer

Her exercise selection is already postpartum-sane — dead bug, bird dog, heel
slides, side plank are exactly the right list, and there's no crunching or
loaded twisting anywhere. Two things the *tempo* format specifically adds, and
one screen:

1. **Breathe out on the effort.** A slow, long set means a longer window of
   raised intra-abdominal pressure, so the exhale cue matters more here than it
   would on fast reps. The coach can say it on the lift beat of the loaded
   moves (RDL, thrust, squat, press) — one word, no nagging.
2. **Stop rules**, shown once as a card and never again unless tapped:
   coning/doming along the midline, any leaking, or a dragging/heavy feeling in
   the pelvis = drop the load or lengthen the tempo. Not a medical screen —
   just the three signs worth knowing.
3. She should have an OB/pelvic-floor clear for loaded training. Ask once at
   setup, store the answer, never ask again.

## 3. UI direction

**DIRECTION CHANGED 2026-08-05 — dark, not light.** The original brief was a
light/bone canvas. Gabe's better instinct: *keep KILOS exactly as it is, but hot
pink instead of black.* That's the call. Rationale and the rejected light
option are both kept below.

Reference poster #2 (`GIRLS JUST WANNA RUN`) is already this: a deep wine ground
with electric pink type. It was never a light design.

### 3.1 Three readings of "pink instead of black"

| | Approach | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Pink canvas** — the `#222` background becomes saturated magenta | ❌ White text on `#FF1E8E` is only **3.6:1** — fails AA for body text. It would force black type everywhere, and a full-screen saturated magenta is fatiguing at gym brightness. |
| **B** | **Same dark canvas, pink accents** — swap every accent to magenta | ⚠️ Cheapest, but it reads as *Gabe's app wearing a different jersey*. Not hers. |
| **C** | **Pink-tinted dark canvas + magenta accent** — the black itself goes plum/wine | ✅ **This one.** Distinctly hers, poster-accurate, and still a token swap. |

### 3.2 Tokens — the plum-dark system

| Token | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#14060E` | near-black with a plum tint — reads warm, not neutral |
| `--surface` | `#1F0A16` | cards, one step up |
| `--ink` | `#FFF5FA` | warm white, faintly pink |
| `--ink-soft` | `#C9A8B8` | labels, secondary — dusty rose, not grey |
| `--hot` | `#FF1E8E` | the magenta. Timer, phase words, active state |
| `--ember` | `#FF6B2C` | poster #1's orange — celebration only |
| `--line` | `rgba(255,30,142,.18)` | hairlines are tinted pink, never grey |

Contrast, checked:

- `--hot` on `--bg` → **5.5:1** ✅ passes AA for normal text
- `--ink` on `--bg` → **~20:1** ✅
- White on solid `--hot` → **3.6:1** ❌ — so magenta stays a *text/stroke*
  colour here. Where it must be a fill (buttons, the active block), the label
  on it is **black**, which gets 5.8:1.

The tint is what does the work: swapping neutral grey for plum in the canvas,
the hairlines, and the soft text is the difference between "her app" and "his
app with pink buttons."

### 3.3 Why this is the better call

- **It's a token swap, not a rebuild.** §3.5 below was a whole phase of work
  that now largely disappears — every shadow, overlay, timer ring and focus
  state in KILOS already works on a dark ground.
- **Everything stays battle-tested.** The dark component set is the one that's
  been used mid-workout for months.
- **Better where she'll actually train** — early morning, evening, a dim room
  with a sleeping baby. A bone-white screen at 5am is hostile.
- **OLED**: a dark canvas is materially cheaper on battery than a full-bleed
  light one.

**What's lost:** the light direction was the bigger visual departure from
KILOS. Two things claw that back — the plum tint (§3.2), and letting the
**finish/share card go light** (§3.4), so the loud bone-and-magenta poster look
still exists, in the one place that's meant to be seen by other people.

### 3.2 Type — reuse, don't buy

No new fonts. The two KILOS already self-hosts do the poster look exactly:

- **Teko** (condensed) — the timer, rep counts, session titles. Huge.
- **Space Mono**, tracked ~0.18em, uppercase — the poster's small type is
  literally this (`YEAR 02`, `ATTN ::`, `CH.0 FOR TIER ZERO`). Every micro-label
  in her app.

That's a familial-but-distinct identity with zero extra download and zero cold-
start flash.

### 3.4 The moments

- **Home** — plum-dark canvas, one magenta block for the next session, and a
  tracked-mono strip in poster grammar: `WK 04 · LOWER A · 34 MIN`.
- **Player** — the number owns the screen. Timer in Teko at display size, the
  phase word (`LOWER` / `SQUEEZE`) in magenta beneath it, everything else gone.
  The canvas **warms toward magenta on the work phase and cools back to plum on
  rest**, so she reads the *colour* from across the room, not the text — the
  dark version of the original light/magenta flip, and it works better because
  the shift is a glow rather than a flash.
- **Finish** — **the one light screen.** Bone canvas, halftone/motion-blur hero,
  numbers in tracked mono. It's the screen that gets screenshotted and sent, so
  it's where the poster look belongs. Going light here also makes it feel like a
  *reward* — the app opens the curtains once, at the end.

### 3.5 The light-canvas option (rejected, kept for the record)

The original plan was a bone `#FAF7F5` canvas with `#141414` ink and `#C40C6B`
for magenta text. It was rejected in favour of §3.2 because it meant rebuilding
the entire component set — shadows, hairlines, overlays, the timer ring and
every focus state all need genuinely different designs on light, and none of
that work is shared with the tested dark set.

If it's ever revived, build it in *her* stylesheet rather than threading a theme
through `src/style.css`, which would put Gabe's app at risk for zero benefit.
The surviving piece of it is the finish card in §3.4.

## 4. Architecture

**A sibling sub-app in this repo** — same pattern as `tomato/` and `tayo/`: her
own page, her own icon, her own localStorage namespace, sharing the engine.

```
hotmum/index.html            her shell (own manifest, own icons)
src/hotmum/program.js        exercises + the 3 sessions, time-based
src/hotmum/player.js         the guided-session UI (~700 lines)
src/hotmum/style.css         light + magenta
public/voice-hotmum/         her coach clips
```

**Shared, not copied** — these are already pure and DOM-free, which is what
makes this cheap:

- `buildStepQueue`, `sessionOverview`, `tempoStateAt`, `estimateSessionSecs`
  from [rehab.js](src/workout/rehab.js) — the whole timed-session engine
- [tempoCues.js](src/workout/tempoCues.js) — what the coach says on each beat
- `streak.js`, `progression.js`
- `scripts/generate-voice.mjs` (needs a `--voice` flag added)

**Not shared:** [main.js](src/main.js) is 7,553 lines wired to `index.html`'s
DOM and carries CrossFit, coaches, legends, WODs, muscle maps, share cards —
none of which she needs. Her player is a fresh, much smaller shell over the same
engine.

**One refactor first (§P2):** lift the engine functions out of `rehab.js` into
`src/workout/stepEngine.js` and re-export them from `rehab.js` so nothing
breaks. Mechanical, and `tests/unit/rehab.test.js` proves it. Without it, every
change she needs lands in the file Gabe's daily rehab depends on.

**Units: pounds.** Her whole plan is in lb; KILOS is kg. Her app is lb-native —
a `unit` field on the profile, formatted at the edge. (Yes: the app called
KILOS has a sibling that doesn't use kilos. Another point for a new name.)

**Sync: skip for v1.** localStorage only, `sam-*` key prefix. Supabase can come
later; it's a background concern and shouldn't gate her using it next week.

### Why not Scottish (asked and closed, 2026-08-05)

Scottish needs a paid plan. Three doors, all shut on free:

- Listing/searching voices → `401 missing_permission: voices_read` (the key is
  TTS-scoped; a full-scope key fixes this one)
- Using a **library** voice via API → `402 paid_plan_required`
- **Voice Design** (describe a voice in words, get a custom one) →
  `403 feature_not_available — creating a voice through the API is only
  available on a paid plan`

The free default roster has English (Alice, Lily) but no Scottish female.

Quota was never the blocker — voice *access* was. The whole clip pack is only
~2,000–2,500 characters, well inside the free monthly allowance.

Free Scottish routes were checked and rejected:

- **Apple system voices** — Fiona (the old Scottish voice) is not shipped on
  this macOS and nothing is on disk; only Daniel (`en_GB`, male) is installed.
- **Piper / local open-source neural TTS** — does have Scottish English, and is
  genuinely free. Rejected: a second audio pipeline to maintain, a voice
  character that doesn't match the rest of the stack, and audibly lower quality
  than the ElevenLabs pack for a voice she hears hundreds of times a month.

If Scottish ever becomes worth revisiting, one month of the entry paid tier
generates the pack permanently (clips are files — they don't expire with the
subscription), via either a library voice or Voice Design. The design prompt is
drafted and kept here for that day:

> *"A Scottish woman in her early thirties with a warm, natural Glasgow accent.
> She is a strength coach: calm, encouraging and quietly confident, never shouty
> or bubbly. Clear, grounded delivery with an easy smile in the voice."*

A third path stays open regardless: record a real person via the personal-voice
route in [VOICE-RECORDING.md](VOICE-RECORDING.md) — dropping `<slug>.m4a` files
into the voice dir overrides everything, no API needed.

Clips needed: the 23-phrase core pack (get set / go / hold / rest / switch sides
/ lift / squeeze / lower / one–ten / last three / last one / halfway / session
complete) **plus ~20 exercise names** and her form cues. Same pipeline as the
Brian pack — trim, 0.89 peak, AAC 48k mono — output to `public/voice-hotmum/`.
iOS silences `speechSynthesis` mid-session, so **every** spoken phrase must
ship as a clip; a missing one is mute on her iPhone.
