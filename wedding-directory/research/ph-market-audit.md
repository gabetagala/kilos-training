# PH Wedding Web Landscape — Visual & Tone Audit

> Research agent output, 2026-08-17.

## Site audits

**brideandbreakfast.ph** — The editorial gold standard: clean grid, big photography, generous whitespace, neutral/light palette with soft accents. Tone is aspirational-warm English ("Where Filipino Love Stories Meet Local Craftsmanship"), with bylined writers and curated Real Weddings as the trust engine — legitimacy comes from taste and curation, not badges. Vendor money shows up as tasteful branded features, not banner clutter. Mobile-responsive but built for lean-back browsing, not decision-making.

**nuptials.ph** — Magazine-like, grey/white minimalism, photography-first, SEO-guide tone ("The Ultimate Wedding Guide"). Professional but bloodless; vendor listings are thumbnail-and-blurb with no reviews or prices. Competent, forgettable.

**kasal.com** — The dated incumbent: early-2010s directory bones, white/black/blue-link palette, GIF spacers, and repeating banner ads (Splendido, Hizon's) interrupting everything. Tone is actually charming Taglish ("Bakit MASAYA MagpaKASAL sa PAMPANGA") and it does list package pricing — but the clutter, dropdown-driven search, and absent reviews read as "brochure rack," not tool. Its Taglish warmth is worth stealing; nothing else is.

**vowly-ph.com / wedplanner.ph** — The AI-template tells, cataloged: emoji-bullet feature lists; the "Stop juggling. Start planning." two-sentence-imperative headline formula; numbered "How it works" sections; surname-less testimonial couples ("Maria & Carlo") with fragment quotes ("Ang dali lang pala!"); suspiciously tidy vanity metrics ("4,120+ weddings, 412,000+ guests"); admitted placeholders ("Real couple quotes coming soon"); forced-organic Tagalog sprinkled like seasoning ("Your tita's Excel file could never"); zero founder/team identity, zero verifiable social proof. Ironically their pricing pages are transparent — but pesos on a page nobody trusts convert nothing. Lesson: Taglish + emoji without human evidence reads as scam-adjacent.

**Themes & Motifs / Getting Married expo** — Legacy trade-show corporate: since 2001, "Wedding Expo Philippines, 44th edition," Top Booker campaigns, Grand Food Tasting, glossy freebie magazine. Their web presence is keyword-stuffed dated SEO (the bridalfairsphilippines.com title tag is 15 keywords long) and their main sites block bots outright. Trust = longevity and SMX floor space, not the web. Digital is their soft flank.

**GCash / Angkas (the "legit" benchmark)** — GCash: clean white space, blue/teal, English UI with Tagalog reserved for *emotional* moments, and hard trust anchors ("regulated by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas," "94M Filipinos"). Angkas: bold orange, playful Taglish ("Kabayan, excited ka na ba sa unang Angkas mo?") sitting on top of hard numbers ("99.997% safety record"). The shared formula for "legit": **restrained modern UI + one regulated/quantified proof point + warmth in the copy, never in the chrome**. Scam-y is the inverse: loud gradients, emoji chrome, unverifiable numbers.

## Recommendations

**(a) Tone.** Taglish, kaibigan-casual — but structured like GCash: English for data and UI labels (prices, capacity, filters stay in clean English), Tagalog for warmth and empathy in headlines and empty states. "PM sent po" jokes land *very* well — DTI itself memed that "PM is the key" is an "evil sellers" practice and it violates RA 7394's price-display rule. A price-transparency directory joking "walang PM-PM dito, nasa page na ang presyo" is an in-joke that doubles as the value proposition and puts you on the couple's (and the regulator's) side. One rule: the joke lives in marketing copy, never inside vendor listings — listings stay factual.

**(b) Facebook conventions to mirror.** Couples' mental model is FB groups + pages, so borrow: star ratings *with visible reviewer names and dates* (anonymous ratings read fake); "Typically replies within X hours" and last-active signals; a message-first CTA (deep-link to Messenger/Viber — don't force email or in-app chat nobody checks); photo albums as the primary listing content, captioned and dated like a page's uploads; and comment-style Q&A under listings ("HM po ang 100 pax?") with public answers. Public answers to "HM?" is the FB ritual, subverted.

**(c) Filipino-without-cliché motifs.** Restraint means texture, not icons: piña/jusi fabric weave as a subtle background texture; capiz-shell translucency as a card/overlay treatment; the warm golden-hour palette of PH garden weddings (sampaguita white, capiz cream, deep leaf green, one warm gold accent) instead of imported blush-pink; barong embroidery as a hairline border pattern. Filipino should be felt in the light and materials, never spelled out in symbols.

**(d) Naming.** Yes, there's a pattern: two-syllable, plain-spoken Tagalog nouns/verbs that state the job — Angkas (ride pillion), Kumu ("kumusta"), Bantay (watch over). Not cute, not baybayin-mystical: everyday words a tita can say. For this product, that vocabulary is words of settling and promising — e.g. *Tanan*, *Pili*, *Presyo*-adjacent or *Sagot* ("Kami na ang sagot") territory. Test: does it survive being said out loud in a Messenger voice note without explanation? GCash proves an English hybrid also works, but the Tagalog-noun route is the one that earns "gawang Pinoy" affection.
