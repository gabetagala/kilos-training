# First 13 Weeks of Solids — worked example

> A concrete template built from [FEEDING.md](FEEDING.md), using food we
> actually cook. Roughly **6.0 → 9.0 months**.
>
> **This is a template, not a prescription.** Start when *he's* ready (sits with
> support, steady head, swallows rather than pushes out, reaches for our food) —
> that may be a few weeks either side of 6 months, and the calendar shifts with
> him, not the other way round. Run it past his pediatrician, especially the
> allergen weeks. Milk (breast or formula) stays his main nutrition for the
> whole of this period; solids are practice and iron, not replacement.

---

## The four phases at a glance

| Phase | Weeks | Age | The one job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Taste breadth** | 1–2 | ~6.0 mo | A different vegetable every single day |
| **2. Iron + first allergens** | 3–6 | ~6.5 mo | Iron-rich food daily; egg and peanut in |
| **3. Remaining allergens** | 7–10 | ~7.5 mo | All 9 allergens introduced and rotating |
| **4. Texture** | 11–13 | ~8.5 mo | Off smooth purée, onto lumps and finger food |

Three rules that run across all 13 weeks:

1. **One new allergen per meal, in the morning**, at home, when he's well, with
   two hours free to watch him. Tip of the spoon → wait 10 minutes → carry on.
2. **Once an allergen is in, it stays in — at least once a week, forever.**
   Introducing and dropping is its own risk.
3. **Never pressure.** We decide what and when; he decides whether and how much.
   A refused food is offered again another day, not negotiated over.

## Phase 1 — Weeks 1–2: taste breadth

**Amount:** 1–2 teaspoons, once a day, after a milk feed. He will eat almost
none of it. That's fine — this phase is about *taste exposure*, not nutrition.

**Why a different vegetable daily instead of one food for three days:** the
frequency of change is the active ingredient. This is the only feeding
intervention with effects still measurable at age 6.

**No allergens yet.** Two weeks of plain food teaches us what his normal
gagging, coughing and face-pulling look like — so that when egg arrives in week
3 we can tell a reaction from a texture problem.

### Week 1 — smooth purée, one vegetable per day

| Day | Food | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Kalabasa (squash) | Sweet, easy first taste |
| Tue | Kamote (sweet potato) | Thin with breast milk or water |
| Wed | Sayote | Mild |
| Thu | Carrot | |

| Sat | Upo | |

### Week 2 — keep rotating; add the vitamin C habit

| Day | Food | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Talong (eggplant) | Peeled, well-cooked |
| Tue | Broccoli | Slightly bitter — expect faces, offer again |
| Wed | Baguio beans | |
| Thu | Cauliflower | |
| Fri | Gabi (taro) | Fully cooked |
| Sat | Kalabasa + malunggay | First green in |
| Sun | Papaya | First fruit — after 13 vegetables, deliberately |

**Expect rejection, and keep going.** Evidence says **8–10+ exposures** before a
new food is accepted; surveyed parents give up after **3–5**. A face-pull is
data about the moment, not a verdict on the food. Log it and re-offer next week.

## Phase 2 — Weeks 3–6: iron, then egg and peanut

**Amount:** building from 1 to 2–3 tablespoons, moving from one meal a day to
two by week 6.

**The iron switch flips here.** His birth iron stores are running out and he
needs ~11 mg/day. This is where lugaw stops being plain — plain lugaw is
0.45 mg iron; lugaw with chicken liver is 10.20 mg.

### The weekly iron rhythm (carries on for the rest of the year)

| Day | Iron source | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Chicken liver** | 1–2 tsp puréed into lugaw |
| Tue | **Dilis** | Deboned, mashed fine into lugaw |
| Wed | **Egg** | Well-cooked, mashed |
| Thu | **Fish** (tilapia / bangus) | Flaked, bones checked twice |
| Fri | **Chicken liver** | Second and *last* liver day this week |
| Sat | **Monggo + malunggay** | Soak the monggo overnight — cuts phytate 45% |
| Sun | **Ground pork or beef** | Finely puréed |

**Liver caps at twice a week** — it's the one food where more is worse, because
of vitamin A. Everything else is animal-source-food-daily, which is one of
WHO's few strong recommendations.

Pair the plant-iron days (monggo, malunggay) with calamansi or papaya. Real
effect, just don't over-bank on it.

### Allergen schedule

| Week | New allergen | Form | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3** | **Egg** | **Well-cooked only** — hard-boiled, mashed. Never raw or runny | Full 2 hours |
| **4** | **Peanut** | 2 tsp peanut butter thinned with 2–3 tsp hot water, cooled, stirred into purée. **Never off a spoon, never a lump** | Full 2 hours |
| **5** | **Cow's milk** (Tue), **Wheat** (Fri) | Plain unsweetened yogurt · soft-cooked pasta or bread fingers | 2 hours each |
| **6** | **Soy** (Tue), **Fish** (Fri) | Tofu / taho without syrup · flaked tilapia | 2 hours each |

Egg and peanut go first because they carry by far the strongest evidence — egg
RR 0.60, peanut RR 0.31, both high-certainty. Everything introduced enters the
weekly rotation immediately and never leaves.

**Not soy sauce for soy** — almost no protein and an enormous salt load. Tofu or
plain taho.

## Phase 3 — Weeks 7–10: finish the allergens, start the lumps

**Amount:** two meals a day moving toward three. Texture shifts from smooth
purée to **mashed with soft lumps** — this is the start of the one real
deadline in the literature.

| Week | New allergen | Form | Texture goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7** | **Sesame** | Tahini or hummus thinned into purée | Mashed, not blended |
| **8** | **Tree nut** | Cashew or walnut butter, thinned. **No whole or chopped nuts before 4 years** | Visible soft lumps |
| **9** | **Shellfish** | Shrimp, cooked and finely chopped | Soft finger foods appear |
| **10** | — none new — | Rotation + variety | Mixed textures in one bowl |

**By the end of week 10 all nine allergens are in** — egg, peanut, cow's milk,
wheat, soy, fish, sesame, tree nut, shellfish — and each is being served at
least weekly.

**Week 10 is a deliberate consolidation week.** No new allergens; keep rotating
vegetables and re-offering the ones he rejected in weeks 1–2. Many of them land
on the second or third pass.

## Phase 4 — Weeks 11–13: texture, and eating what we eat

**The deadline:** lumpy textures should be established **by 8–10 months**.
Children introduced to lumps after 9 months had more feeding difficulty and ate
fewer fruits and vegetables *at age 7*. This is the phase where the calendar
matters most.

**Amount:** three meals a day, plus finger food he feeds himself.

| Week | Texture | Foods |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | Soft finger foods alongside mashed | Steamed kamote sticks, ripe saging spears, soft-cooked kalabasa batons |
| **12** | Coarser mash, more self-feeding | Soft pasta pieces, tofu cubes, flaked fish, well-cooked monggo |
| **13** | Adapted family food | Whatever we're eating — **portioned out before salt, patis or toyo goes in** |

The last row is the real goal. Shared family meals where everyone eats the same
food is protective against picky eating, and children eat what they watch us
eat — parental modelling is one of the strongest correlates in the literature,
ahead of simply having the food available.

### Choking rules for this phase

- **No whole nuts before 4.** Not whole, not chopped.
- **No peanut butter in a lump or off a spoon before 4.**
- **Whole grapes, hot-dog-shaped pieces, hard raw vegetables** — halve
  lengthwise or leave out.
- Seated, supervised, not in the car, not distracted.
- **Gagging is loud and protective; choking is silent.** Learning that
  difference beforehand is worth more than any list.

## What "on track" looks like at week 13 (~9 months)

- All **9 allergens** introduced, each served **at least weekly**
- An **iron-rich food every day**, liver capped at twice a week
- Roughly **35–45 distinct foods** logged
- Eating **lumps and soft finger foods**, not just purée
- Some foods still refused — normal, keep re-offering
- No added salt, no added sugar, no honey, no cow's milk as a drink

---

## What happens next (so it isn't a surprise)

Around **12–24 months** he will very likely start refusing food he previously
loved. That is **food neophobia**, it peaks between ages 2 and 6, it is roughly
72% heritable, and it is not caused by anything we did.

In the variety study, the early-variety advantage was **statistically invisible
at 15 months and at 3 years** — and then clearly present again **at age 6**. The
work done in weeks 1–2 is banked, not visible.

One number worth remembering when that phase hits: parental *worry* about a
fussy 15-month-old raised the odds of genuine picky eating at 38 months from
**17% to 50%**. The calm is part of the intervention.

## Open questions for the pediatrician

1. **Does he have severe eczema?** If yes — or if he already reacts to egg —
   NIAID says consider peanut IgE or skin-prick testing *before* week 4.
   Guidelines genuinely disagree on whether this is needed; his skin decides it.
2. **Vitamin D** — 400 IU/day is the AAP standard for breastfed infants; we
   couldn't find settled PH-specific guidance.
3. **Free DOH micronutrient powder** for 6–23 months (60 sachets at 6–11
   months) — worth asking for rather than buying supplements.
4. **Liver frequency** — twice weekly is our conservative read on the vitamin A
   ceiling; no PH-specific infant guidance found.
5. **High-nitrate vegetables** (spinach, beets) in early infancy — we did not
   research this and are not making a claim either way. Ask before making them
   a staple.
